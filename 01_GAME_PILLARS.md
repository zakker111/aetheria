# 01 — Game Pillars

## Fantasy
The player is a visible-but-remote god. The world continues acting without the player.

## Pillars
1. **Autonomous people** — people have needs, goals, skills, relationships and memory.
2. **Emergent societies** — settlements and factions are consequences of people interacting, not scripted scenes.
3. **Physical world matters** — terrain, water, resources and destruction change what societies can do.
4. **God as influence, not RTS cursor** — the player may help, harm, reshape and inspire, but does not normally issue direct commands to every person.
5. **Readable simulation** — the player can inspect why people do things.
6. **Persistent consequences** — important actions create memories, reputations, ownership changes, migration, rebuilding and history.
7. **Modular complexity** — new mechanics can be added without rewriting the simulation core.

## Player verbs
Observe, inspect, follow, bless, curse, give, remove, reshape terrain, create resources, destroy resources, protect, threaten, inspire, punish, and simply watch.

## Success condition
The game should create stories that the player did not explicitly script:
- a mining town grows beside an ore vein;
- a road changes settlement growth;
- a famine causes migration;
- a leader becomes influential;
- a faction splits after a succession crisis;
- a miracle changes religious belief;
- a war destroys infrastructure and leaves memories that affect later diplomacy.

## Anti-pillar
Do not turn the game into a conventional city builder where the player places every building, assigns every worker and directly controls every battle unit.
